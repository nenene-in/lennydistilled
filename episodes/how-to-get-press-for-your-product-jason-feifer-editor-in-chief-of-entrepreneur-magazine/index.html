How to get press for your product | Jason Feifer (editor in chief of Entrepreneur magazine)

October 12, 2023

Featuring: Jason Fried (Co-Founder and CEO, 37signals)

13 quotes · 8 insights

Watch Full Episode

Your first priority is survival, not optimization

The only thing you can really control are your costs. You can't control the alternatives in the market. The competition, you can't control them. You can't determine the market itself. You can't control the economy.
Jason FriedCo-Founder and CEO, 37signals 01:11:53
Figure out how to do as much as they can on their own before they bring on a second person. Really work and struggle through something on your own. The moment you bring on someone else, you've got another mouth to feed.
Jason FriedCo-Founder and CEO, 37signals 01:12:39
The reason I think it's great for entrepreneurs to start bootstrapping is because they just have more practice making money, and they get better, and better, and better at the fundamental skill you need to have ultimately to run a successful business, which is to make money.
Jason FriedCo-Founder and CEO, 37signals 00:00:00

Reality trumps your model every time

I am not driven by data, or I don't have different P&Ls for each product in a way where we're looking at them very carefully. It's in total, collectively, are we making more money than we spend? That's the only thing we ultimately look at.
Jason FriedCo-Founder and CEO, 37signals 00:15:37

Small can be the goal, not just a phase

This is a way to be. It's an alternative to what you're often hearing in our industry, which is, 'Go big or go home. Raise a bunch of money and get huge, and unicorn status,' and the whole thing. That's a way. Just know though that basically almost nobody makes it that way.
Jason FriedCo-Founder and CEO, 37signals 00:00:00
Small is not just a stepping stone, small is a great destination itself.
Jason FriedCo-Founder and CEO, 37signals 00:13:55

Small focused teams outperform large coordinated ones

We build products with two people at a time. So every feature we work on in Basecamp, or HEY, or whatever we're building is two people, one programmer, one designer, and they have a maximum of six weeks to deliver the feature that they're working on.
Jason FriedCo-Founder and CEO, 37signals 00:11:58

Simplicity beats feature competition

You don't need to outdo the competition, it's expensive and defensive. Underdo your competition. We need more simplicity and clarity.
Jason FriedCo-Founder and CEO, 37signals 00:14:12

Data informs but doesn't decide

Give your intuition credit. Give your gut credit. It's absorbed everything. And plus the things that you don't know it absorbed that are valuable.
Jason FriedCo-Founder and CEO, 37signals 00:46:01
I think everything's a judgment call. And so yeah, data can play a part. All sorts of things can play a part, but unless you're letting a machine make the decision, that's purely rational. If you're asking a human to make it's a judgment call.
Jason FriedCo-Founder and CEO, 37signals 00:44:15

Feel it before you build it

How does this feel? Is something we talk more about at work than pretty much anything else, which is like, how does this screen feel? How does this flow feel? How does this feature feel?
Jason FriedCo-Founder and CEO, 37signals 00:47:43

Time constraints force better decisions

This is not an estimate of time, it's an appetite, which is a radically different approach. An estimate is like how long do we think it's going to take? We instead have appetites. Our appetite for any individual feature is no more than six weeks. Essentially that's our budget we're willing to spend.
Jason FriedCo-Founder and CEO, 37signals 00:33:36
